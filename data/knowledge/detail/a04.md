TACS-RF: Tool-driven Agentic Circuit Synthesis of

Abstract—We present the framework of TACS-RF — a Tooldriven Agentic Circuit Synthesis of RF and mmWave modules.
In this framework, a large-language-model (LLM) coding agent
(Claude Opus 4.8) directly drives industry-standard electronicdesign-automation (EDA) tools such as Cadence Virtuoso/Spectre,
Calibre, Cadence EMX, and Ansys HFSS to design RF and
mmWave modules. We validate the framework through the
agentic design of a 240–251 GHz sub-THz transmitter in a 90 nm
SiGe BiCMOS process. The agent semi-autonomously designed
the sub-blocks including a 60 GHz push-push Colpitts VCO
with second-harmonic extraction, multistage differential cascode
power drivers, a push-push frequency doubler, a differential
bowtie antenna, and all the interstage matching networks. The
transmitter chain with parasitic-extracted devices and EMverified passives delivers +5.1 to +6.3 dBm across an 11 GHz
varactor-only tuning range, phase noise of −80 to −88 dBc/Hz
at 1 MHz offset while consuming a DC power of 361 mW. We
describe the agent-driven design flow and the resulting division
of labor between human and agent, taking a step toward a “textto-ASIC” future for designing RF/mmWave hardware.

Artificial Intelligence (AI) has rapidly reshaped how humans
interact with information, and is increasingly expected to
reshape scientific and engineering practice. In radio-frequency
(RF) and millimeter-wave/sub-terahertz integrated circuit design specifically, algorithmic and AI-assisted approaches such
as deep-learning-enabled inverse design of multi-port passives and integrated circuits [1] and pixelated, multilayered
topology-optimization frameworks for millimeter-wave power
amplifiers [2] have demonstrated that design automation can
compress schematic and layout iteration cycles from weeks
to days, while achieving state-of-the-art performance without
hand-crafted templates. However, these circuit-design reasoning methods are fundamentally contextless, each new design
target requires the underlying optimization to be re-run largely
from scratch, without prior topology knowledge, circuit-design
reasoning or engineering heuristics from prior work. Large language models (LLMs), in contrast, are distinguished by their ability to reason over contextualized, cumulative knowledge such as retrieving relevant topologies,
applying design equations, and adapting prior solutions to
new specifications rather than re-discovering them numerically. Early studies have begun to demonstrate this potential

transmitter achieving an output power of +5.1 to +6.3 dBm

the tuning range. Through TACS-RF, design time and human
labor can be reduced from months to a few days. Beyond
this manuscript, we envision a “text-to-ASIC” future where
RF/mmWave circuit design, layout, and optimization tasks are
reduced to prompt engineering and simulation verification.

II. LLM–EDA TOOL INTEGRATION
The TACS-RF framework was designed using Enterprise
Claude Opus 4.8 from Anthropic as the LLM agent that
directly controls the required electronic-design-automation
(EDA) tools through scripts, command-line interfaces, and
machine-readable simulation outputs. In this section, we describe the workflow used to connect the agent to the various
software tools that constitute the design stack. The agent
was provided with the design specifications, prior sub-THz
circuit literature, and access to Cadence Virtuoso/Spectre,
Calibre, Cadence EMX, and Ansys HFSS, and was tasked
with autonomously driving each of these tools to completion,
with human involvement limited to result validation, design
feedback, final verification, and top-chip integration. Figure 2
summarizes the resulting agent-driven design flow. A. LLM–Virtuoso Interface The agent controlled Cadence Virtuoso using virtuosobridge-lite [5], an agent-oriented interface for circuit design.
The bridge provides functions for generating schematics and
symbols, checking connectivity, creating simulation views,
launching Spectre simulations, and drawing parameterized
layouts. It also returns simulator logs, operating-point data, and
waveform measurements in machine-readable form, allowing
the agent to evaluate each design iteration and determine
the next parameter update. In our work, two complementary
Spectre-based flows were used: (i) a scripted netlist flow for
rapid parallel sweeps of device sizes, bias conditions, and
passive values, and (ii) a Virtuoso cellview/Maestro flow for
generating schematics, symbols, testbenches, and configuration views. The former supported fast iterative optimization,
whereas the latter was used for human verification. B. LLM Control of Electromagnetic Simulators The agent used Cadence EMX to design the on-chip transformers and inductors as parameterized single-loop octagonal
structures in the top two metal layers. The multiport Sparameter models of the EM simulations were passed back to
Cadence Virtuoso for circuit-level evaluation. The geometry
was iteratively updated based on the transmitter performance
from Virtuoso simulation. Additionally, an on-chip bowtie antenna was designed by
the agent in Ansys HFSS through agent-generated PyAEDT
scripts using the technology stackup and material properties.
The agent generated the antenna layout, defined the ports,
radiation boundaries, meshing, and frequency sweep, and
exported the resulting matching, gain, and directivity data.

## C. Calibre-Based Physical Verification and Extraction

The agent also automated the Calibre design-rule checking (DRC), layout-versus-schematic (LVS), and parasiticextraction flows through command-line execution and scripted
runset configuration. Parameterized transistor layouts were
provided by the human designer. The agent iteratively verified
DRC, LVS, and extracted the resistance, capacitance and
coupling-capacitance (RCC) parasitics of the transistor layout
and imported the RCC-view for performance evaluation.

Using the TACS-RF framework, a sub-THz transmitter
chain with parasitic-extracted devices and EM-verified passives was designed in under a week, compared with 2–3
months for a comparable manual design flow. The primary
bottleneck was EM simulation runtime; a library of precharacterized passives would reduce design time further. With
established design guidelines, prior schematics, and reference
layouts as the agent knowledge base, we expect the workflow
to extend to a variety of RF/mmWave modules. Because
the agent iterates semi-autonomously across schematic, EM,
and extraction tools, the human role shifts from execution to
system definition, ideation, and verification. This can lower
the barrier to entry for RF/mmWave IC design, shorten design
cycles, and reduce engineering effort. As LLMs continue to
improve in reasoning and coding proficiency, we expect fully
autonomous “text-to-ASIC” design to become achievable.
